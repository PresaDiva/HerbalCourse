# Herbal Course

A 35-lesson course on science-backed herbal and natural remedies, written for a new herbalist with no science background who wants real mechanisms, not just high-level summaries.

One lesson is added per day. Each takes about 10 minutes to read.

## What you will learn

| Week | Theme | Lessons |
|------|-------|---------|
| 1 | The toolkit | Molecules and shape, why plants make medicinal chemicals, receptors, enzymes, and what happens to a molecule in the body |
| 2 | Judging evidence | What "natural" means, types of evidence, reading a study, regulation (US vs Ireland and EU), plant part vs extract vs standardization |
| 3 | Safety first | Herb-drug interactions, liver risks, who needs extra caution, quality and testing seals, a personal checklist |
| 4 | Strong evidence | Peppermint oil, ginger, psyllium, St John's wort, topical capsaicin |
| 5 | Promising but mixed | Turmeric, echinacea and elderberry, chamomile/lavender/valerian, garlic, cranberry |
| 6 | Myth-busting | Saw palmetto, ginkgo, "detox" and "immune boosting", placebo effects, spotting hype |
| 7 | In practice | Teas vs tinctures vs capsules vs oils, growing herbs safely, talking to your doctor, building your own reference, capstone |

## How each lesson works

- **Recall questions** on earlier lessons (answers hidden at the end)
- **Evidence grade** for each herb: Strong, Promising, Mixed, Disproven, or Traditional use only
- **How it works**: the active molecule, its target in the body, and the step-by-step chain of events, explained from zero with everyday analogies. Proven knowledge is separated from hypothesis.
- **Safety and interactions**
- **Visuals**: credited photos (Wikimedia Commons), mechanism diagrams, comparison tables
- **Hands-on exercises** on Fridays
- **Terms** defined in plain language
- **Sources**, ranked by tier:
  1. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses
  2. Randomized controlled trials
  3. Regulator and health-body assessments (EMA, WHO, NIH NCCIH, Memorial Sloan Kettering)
  4. Lab and animal studies (used only to explain mechanism)
  5. Traditional use (context only)

Every link is checked before it is cited. Claims that cannot be verified are labeled "unverified" and never support a grade.

## Reading the lessons

Lessons are in [`lessons/`](lessons/) as self-contained HTML pages with light and dark mode. Download the file and open it in any browser. GitHub shows HTML files as code, so use "Download raw file" rather than viewing in the page.

Progress, terms covered, and evidence grades so far are tracked in [`progress.md`](progress.md).

## Disclaimer

This course is education, not medical advice. Herbs can interact with medicines and may not be suitable in pregnancy, before surgery, or for children. Check with a doctor or pharmacist before trying anything new.

Photos are used under free licenses with credit given in each lesson.
